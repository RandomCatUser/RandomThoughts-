When I Fly Towards You

How When I Fly Towards You became more than just a favorite show it became an anchor for my emotions, code, and creative life.

I first pressed the play button on When I Fly Towards You back in 2023 and had no idea how that one click would change the way I create, code, and see the world. I truly think it's the single best thing I have ever watched; what felt like just a breezy and heartfelt youth romance following Su Zaizai and Zhang Lurang soon became so much more an aesthetic, an anchor for my emotions, a constant companion, and something I revisit to the tune of probably 30 rewatches to this day, discovering new levels of comfort with every small background detail and micro expression within.

The Story Behind the Show

For those who haven't yet fallen into it, When I Fly Towards You (当我飞奔向你) is a 2023 Chinese coming of age romantic comedy directed by Mao Deshu, adapted from Zhu Yi's web novel She's a Little Crazy, and originally aired on Youku across 24 short, perfectly paced episodes from June 13 to June 29, 2023 it has since found a global home on Netflix and YouTube. The setup is deceptively simple: in the autumn of 2012, a cheerful transfer student named Su Zaizai (Zhang Miaoyi) arrives at Yucai High School and falls in love at first sight with Zhang Lurang (Zhou Yiran), a brilliant but aloof classmate who lives behind a thick shell of self-doubt, crushed for years under the weight of a controlling mother and constant comparisons to his genius younger brother. What follows is not a story of grand gestures but of small, stubborn persistence Zaizai's sunny, unrelenting warmth slowly coaxing Lurang out of his shell, alongside their friends Gu Ran (Bian Tianyang), Jiang Jia (Jiang Zhinan), and Guan Fang (Guo Zhe), who together form one of the most genuinely lived-in friend groups I've ever seen on screen.

Part of the allure of the series is how immaculately it executes the trope of character growth, especially the healing and growth you witness through its story I swear watching Su Zaizai's unwavering chase of the sun and seeing how not just by luck or chance, but methodically tear down the walls built by a toxic, comparative home life, is one of the most joyous things I've ever watched. And you witness so many turning points in emotional depth, from when Lurang finally stands up to the controlling hand of his mom, to how Zaizai's quiet and steady loyalty pulls him out of low points throughout school when he's panicking over schoolwork or his own insecurities; their smooth transition from high school to college is executed flawlessly, with no reliance on tropes or cheap, manufactured misunderstandings or toxic third act breakups with that deeply satisfying resolution when you see them married, two lifelong partners who get to live the future they've always planned together. Even the characters at Yucai High School, or later in university, just feel so real, to the point where when watching their conversations or their time together as a group it felt like stepping onto the screen myself and joining Fang, Jia, and Ran and their study sessions, midnight snacks, or conversations on the roof.

Fuel For Code and Design

The effect that the series had on me didn't stop at passive viewing; rather it filtered through into my creative and professional work, fundamentally how I operate. With any piece of design that I build now, the softness, the bright and comforting aesthetics of the visuals have deeply impacted how I create; I tend to lean towards warmer color schemes, have adopted cleaner minimalist designs, and will make layouts that feel accessible and calming, to emulate walking down the bright, sunny halls in your campus, or opening a window on a crisp summer day.

I've even noticed how every bit of code I write carries that quiet, focused intention and devotion to precision I witnessed through the series as if when debugging problems, or setting up new systems, I'm approaching it with the same gentle focus, turning routine work into art with deliberate strokes.

The Magic That Is Yung Kai's "Blue"

While the drama's own soundtrack opening theme "When I Run To You" by Lin Chenyang and closing theme "I Think I Will" by Yin Luxi carries the same soft, aching tone as the story itself, there is one song that has since become inseparable from the show in my mind: "Blue" by Yung Kai. Released in August 2024, the Canadian artist wrote the song as a direct love letter to When I Fly Towards You, inspired by the emotional depth of its soundtrack and the feelings the story stirred in him, and it has since gone on to gather over a billion streams worldwide. The peaceful, almost otherworldly melody somehow captures that feeling you get when you're just so excited, and falling in love with art and life again. When I hear the opening guitar to the song I find myself back in the high points of the emotional journey the characters take me on as well, and it is the one song that makes me miss everything about When I Fly Towards Youwhile simultaneously embracing the ambition and growth it inspired.

"It contains in it that bittersweet, almost aching in all the right ways feeling of young love as well as that comforting wave of solace that you feel after a hardship, when everything is finally going to be okay."

For how perfectly it captured the feeling of young love and all of the beauty associated with it in the show, Yung Kai's song truly has claimed a significant space in my daily soundtrack as a personal anthem to self reflection and remembering where my passion originated from.

When Art Meets Internet Noise

The use of "Blue" by Yung Kai in creator led edits such as viral clips featuring personalities like IShowSpeed honestly feels like a slap in the face. That delicate, heartbreakingly emotional song used for loud shouting highlights and ultra active meme edits felt disrespectful to the song in general, as if any beautiful art is now readily subject to internet exploitation for viral trends and shock value.

A perfectly crafted song, with so much meaning embedded, had basically just become loud background music to obnoxious clips, for millions of people who haven't even heard the song with the associated storytelling behind it. Despite how internet culture attempted to turn its stunning soundtrack into disposable meme fodder, When I Fly Towards You has remained an absolutely unsullied gem for me a masterpiece of comfort that will always remain an inspiration.
