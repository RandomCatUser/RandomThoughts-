Yoeshi OS: Reimagining the Web as an Operating System

Turning browser tabs into an advanture.

Where It Started

Every good idea needs a spark, and for me, that was a website by Minusha that does something surprisingly fresh. Instead of just clicking from page to page, Minusha's site lets you drag windows around inside your browser, kind of like you’re working inside a miniature operating system. There were smooth animations, and that sense of actually “using” something, not just reading it. I couldn’t get the idea out of my head. How far could you really push this?

I kept poking around and the more time I spent, the more possibilities I saw. Why not interact with a website the way you’d move windows or widgets on a desktop? Why not make things playful and just a bit unpredictable? That was the turning point for me. I decided I’d try building my own take on it.

First Steps: Amminie OS

That led me to my first serious experiment—Amminie OS. It wasn’t meant to be a fully-fledged system, but more of a test and a chance for me to figure things out. I spent a lot of time seeing just how far you can stretch web tech—layering windows, adding animations, moving things around—trying to recapture the energy of that first discovery.

Building Amminie OS taught me a lot. I messed up, fixed things, and learned the ropes of web interfaces in the process. It was a big confidence boost, but more than that, I saw that this was only the starting line. I wanted something that felt even more alive and personal—something that genuinely captured my vision.

From Amminie OS to Yoeshi OS

What came next wasn’t just another version—it was a total reimagining. Yoeshi OS is everything I learned along the way, turned up a notch. I started looking at every feature, every animation, every little detail and asking, “Does this really feel original? Does this belong in my world?”

Yoeshi OS isn’t just a website with windows stapled on; it’s meant to feel like a living, breathing space you can poke, explore, and get lost in.

Building a Desktop in Your Browser

One of my biggest goals is making the lines between website and desktop app disappear. Sure, browsers today are powerful, but we’re still stuck clicking through static pages most of the time. Yoeshi OS throws that idea out the window. Everything happens in a desktop-like space, with multiple windows you can juggle, shuffle, and switch between—just like you would on a real computer.

It’s more than a neat trick. It makes the browser feel familiar in a way that opens up what you can do and how you can do it. It’s not just about reading or watching or scrolling—it’s about interacting.

Designing for Delight & Customization

I can’t help it: I care about how things look and how they move. In Yoeshi OS, we made sure everything perfectly fits, with an obsessive attention to details that makes the interface feel truly crafted. Windows don’t just snap open—they swoop, they glide, they feel alive. Animations aren’t just eye candy; they’re part of how you know what’s happening, and they ensure the experience is intuitive. Beyond the look, an operating system should bend to your will, not the other way around, so you get what feels best—day or night.

Hidden Surprises

Honestly, I just love when software sneaks in little surprises. There are plenty tucked away in Yoeshi OS—easter eggs, oddball interactions, small moments that don’t announce themselves. They’re reminders that software doesn’t have to take itself too seriously.

Where Next?

Yoeshi OS is very much still growing. I see it as a playground—always in progress, always something to add. I’m already dreaming up new apps, richer multitasking, and whole new ways to blur the line between web and apps.

Yoeshi OS isn’t just a tech project for me. It’s my attempt to stretch what a website can be. The web doesn’t have to be flat or boring. It’s a space for imagination, for experiments, for a bit of wonder.
